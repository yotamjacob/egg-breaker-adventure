The story

What happened to Egg Breaker?

If you have been searching for the Facebook game where you smashed eggs with a hammer to collect items, you are not imagining it — and you are not alone. It really did disappear. Here is what happened, and what you can play instead.

The short answer

Egg Breaker was a Facebook game published around 2008 by LabPixies, a studio best known for its widgets and casual social apps. It was retired when the studio wound down its Facebook titles around 2016. No shutdown announcement, no farewell post, no sequel — the app page simply stopped working.

Whatever survived that retirement was finished off by a second, industry-wide event: Adobe discontinued Flash at the end of 2020, and every major browser removed the plugin. Games built on Flash — which is to say most Facebook games of that era — became genuinely unplayable, not merely unpopular. That is why you cannot find a working link today no matter how deep you dig.

"I played the original constantly as a kid. When it vanished, I figured someone would eventually remake it — nobody did. So in 2025 I built it myself."

Why it is so hard to find anything about it

Three things conspired to erase Egg Breaker from the searchable web more thoroughly than most games of its generation:

  • It lived inside Facebook. Facebook app pages were never well-indexed by search engines, and when an app is removed its canvas page returns an error rather than an archived copy. There is no equivalent of a store listing left behind.
  • It was Flash. Flash games cannot be archived by simply saving a web page. Preservation projects such as Flashpoint have rescued tens of thousands of titles, but Facebook-only games that required a logged-in social session are among the hardest to capture — the game could not run without the platform around it.
  • The name is generic. "Egg breaker" also describes kitchen tools, Easter apps and a dozen unrelated mobile clones released since. Genuine memories of the original get buried under noise.

The result is a game a lot of people distinctly remember playing and almost nobody can prove existed — which is exactly the kind of thing that sends you searching years later.

What the original actually played like

The loop was disarmingly simple, and that was the point. A tray of eggs appeared. You spent a hammer to crack one. Out came gold, a multiplier, a bonus — or, if you were lucky, a collection item you did not have yet. Fill a collection and you unlocked the next stage, with a new theme and a new set of things to find.

Hammers regenerated over time, so a session had a natural end. There was no story and nothing to fail. The pull was entirely the gap between "I have 6 of 7 items" and having all 7 — the same itch that makes sticker albums work.

The revival: Egg Smash Adventures

Nobody remade it, so a solo developer did. Egg Smash Adventures is a fan revival that rebuilds the original loop from scratch in HTML5 — no Flash, no Facebook login, no install. It runs in a browser tab and on Android.

It keeps what made the original work and drops what did not age well:

  • The same core loop — smash eggs, roll prizes, complete a collection, unlock the next stage.
  • 6 monkey companions, each with their own themed world, 8–9 stages and its own collection to finish.
  • 353 collectible items in total, from Common up to the mythical Century Egg tier.
  • No ads, no energy timers, no paywalls. Hammers regenerate; nothing asks you to wait eight hours or watch a video.
  • Cloud save via Google, so progress survives a reinstall or a switch between phone and desktop.

It is a spiritual successor rather than a copy — the art, the worlds and the items are all new. What is faithful is the feel: crack an egg, watch the prize land, get slightly too invested in finishing a set.

Frequently asked

Is the original Egg Breaker coming back?

No. The rights sit with a studio that no longer operates the title, the code was Flash, and there has been no sign of an official revival in the years since. Egg Smash Adventures is an independent fan project, not an official continuation.

Is this the same game I played?

Not literally. It is a rebuild of the same idea by someone who played the original for years. If you remember the tray of eggs, the hammer counter and the collection album, you will recognise the rhythm immediately — but the worlds and items are new.

Does it cost anything?

No. It is free on web and on Android. There are optional one-time premium purchases for convenience and cosmetics, and nothing in the game is gated behind them.

Do I need an account?

No. Progress saves to your browser automatically. Signing in with Google is optional and only exists so your save can follow you to another device.
